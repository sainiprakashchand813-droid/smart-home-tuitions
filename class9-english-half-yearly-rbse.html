Class 9 English – Half-Yearly Model Paper

RBSE | Session 2026–27 | Practice Paper with Solutions & Online Test

Important syllabus note: This is an original practice model paper, not an official RBSE-issued paper. Schools may prescribe different half-yearly chapters and marks. The literature portion below follows a commonly used early-session Beehive/Moments selection. Please match it with your school’s 2026–27 syllabus before the exam.

HALF-YEARLY EXAMINATION 2026–27

ENGLISH – CLASS IX (RBSE Practice Set)

Time: 3 Hours | Maximum Marks: 80

General Instructions:

  1. All questions are compulsory unless an internal choice is given.
  3. Marks are indicated against each question.
  4. Attempt the literature questions only from chapters prescribed by your school.

Section A – Reading (15 Marks)

Q1. Read the passage and answer the questions. (8 × 1 = 8)

Books are among the most reliable companions of human beings. They introduce us to new ideas, places and people. Reading regularly improves vocabulary, concentration and the ability to express thoughts. A good reader does not merely collect information; he or she learns to question, compare and think independently. In the digital age, information is available everywhere, but careful reading is still necessary to separate facts from rumours. Students should therefore develop the habit of reading newspapers, biographies, stories and informative articles every day.

  1. What do books introduce us to?
  2. Mention any two benefits of regular reading.
  3. What does a good reader learn to do?
  4. Why is careful reading important in the digital age?
  5. Name two kinds of reading material suggested in the passage.
  6. Find a word in the passage meaning “dependable”.
  7. Write the opposite of “carefully” (as an adverb) is not required; give the opposite of “independent”.
  8. Suggest a suitable title for the passage.
Q2. Read the information and answer. (7 × 1 = 7)

A school organised a “Green Week”. Class IX planted 35 saplings, Class VIII collected 42 kg of paper for recycling, and Class VII prepared 28 posters on saving water. The programme lasted five days. The principal announced that the class showing the best care for its plants would receive a certificate.

  1. How many saplings did Class IX plant?
  2. Which class collected paper?
  3. How much paper was collected?
  4. How many posters were prepared?
  5. How long did Green Week last?
  6. What would the winning class receive?
  7. Give one suitable slogan for the programme.

Section B – Writing (15 Marks)

Q3. (5 marks)

Write a letter to your friend describing how you are preparing for your half-yearly examinations. (100–120 words)

OR Write an application to your Principal requesting two days’ leave due to illness.

Q4. (5 marks)

Write a paragraph in 100–120 words on “Importance of Trees”.

OR “A Visit to a Historical Place”.

Q5. (5 marks)

Prepare a notice for your school notice board informing students about an inter-house English debate competition. Include date, time, venue and contact person.

OR Write a short story in 100–120 words beginning: “It was a rainy evening when I heard a knock at the door…”

Section C – Grammar (15 Marks)

Q6. Fill in the blanks with suitable articles/prepositions. (5 × 1 = 5)
  1. He is ___ honest boy.
  2. The book is ___ the table.
  3. She has been waiting ___ morning.
  4. We go to school ___ bus.
  5. Ravi is ___ tallest boy in the class.
Q7. Choose the correct verb form. (5 × 1 = 5)
  1. She ___ (play/plays/playing) tennis every day.
  2. They ___ (was/were/is) absent yesterday.
  3. I ___ (have/has/had) finished my work.
  4. Look! The baby ___ (sleep/sleeps/is sleeping).
  5. If it rains, we ___ (will stay/stayed/stays) at home.
Q8. Do as directed. (5 × 1 = 5)
  1. He is too weak to walk. (Rewrite using “so…that”)
  2. She said, “I am happy.” (Change into indirect speech)
  3. The teacher praised the student. (Change into passive voice)
  4. Rita is taller than Sita. (Change into positive degree)
  5. You are coming, ___? (Add a question tag)

Section D – Literature (35 Marks)

Practice selection: Beehive – The Fun They Had, The Sound of Music, The Little Girl, A Truly Beautiful Mind; poems – The Road Not Taken, Wind, Rain on the Roof. Moments – The Lost Child, The Adventures of Toto, Iswaran the Storyteller. Confirm chapter coverage with your school.

Q9. Answer any five in 30–40 words. (5 × 2 = 10)
  1. Why did Margie hate school in “The Fun They Had”?
  2. How did Evelyn Glennie learn to sense music?
  3. Why was Kezia afraid of her father?
  4. What qualities made Einstein a remarkable person?
  5. What message does the poem “Wind” convey?
  6. What did the child want at the fair in “The Lost Child”?
  7. How did Toto create trouble for the family?
Q10. Answer any three in 60–80 words. (3 × 3 = 9)
  1. Compare the old schools with Margie’s mechanical school.
  2. Describe Bismillah Khan’s contribution to Indian classical music.
  3. How did Kezia’s feelings towards her father change?
  4. Describe Iswaran’s way of telling stories.
Q11. Reference to context – Poetry. (2 × 3 = 6)

Read the following original practice lines inspired by the theme of a poem (not a textbook quotation): “Two paths lay waiting in the wood; / I paused to choose the one I could.”

  1. Which poem in Beehive explores a choice between two roads?
  2. What do the two roads symbolise?
  3. What is the central idea of the poem?
Q12. Answer any two in about 80–100 words. (2 × 5 = 10)
  1. Write a character sketch of Margie or Tommy.
  2. Explain how determination helped Evelyn Glennie overcome her hearing impairment.
  3. Describe the child’s changing feelings in “The Lost Child”.

Section A – Reading

Q1. (1) Books introduce us to new ideas, places and people. (2) Regular reading improves vocabulary and concentration. (3) A good reader learns to question, compare and think independently. (4) Careful reading helps us separate facts from rumours. (5) Newspapers and biographies (stories or informative articles are also correct). (6) Reliable. (7) Dependent. (8) A suitable title is “The Importance of Reading”.

Q2. (1) Class IX planted 35 saplings. (2) Class VIII collected the paper. (3) It collected 42 kg of paper. (4) Class VII prepared 28 posters. (5) Green Week lasted five days. (6) The winning class would receive a certificate. (7) Slogan: “Save the Earth, save our future.”

Section B – Writing

Q3 – Sample informal letter:

25, Shastri Nagar, Jaipur
23 November 2026

Dear Amit,
I hope you are fine. My half-yearly examinations are approaching, so I have prepared a proper study timetable. I study English and Mathematics in the morning and revise Science and Social Science in the evening. I solve textbook questions and practice model papers every week. I also take short breaks to remain fresh. My teachers have advised me to revise regularly and avoid wasting time on mobile phones. I am confident that regular practice will help me perform well. Please share your preparation strategy too.

Yours lovingly,
Rahul

Yours obediently,
Rahul Sharma, Class IX-B

Q4 – Sample paragraph: Importance of Trees

Trees are essential for life on Earth. They give us oxygen, fruits, flowers, wood and shade. They absorb carbon dioxide and help keep the air clean. Trees provide shelter to birds and animals and prevent soil erosion. They also help maintain the water cycle and reduce the effects of global warming. Unfortunately, forests are being cut down for roads, buildings and industries. We should plant more trees and protect existing forests. Every student can help by planting a sapling and caring for it. Trees are our natural wealth, and saving them means protecting our future.

OR – A Visit to a Historical Place: Last Sunday, I visited Amer Fort with my family. It is situated near Jaipur and is famous for its beautiful architecture. We saw large gates, courtyards, halls and artistic walls. The guide told us about the history of the fort and the rulers who lived there. The mirror work and paintings were especially attractive. I took photographs and learned many things about Rajasthan’s heritage. The visit was both enjoyable and educational. I realised that we must protect historical monuments for future generations.

OR – Sample story: It was a rainy evening when I heard a knock at the door. I opened it and saw a little boy standing outside, completely wet. He had lost his way while returning from tuition. I brought him inside, gave him a towel and called the number written on his school identity card. Soon, his worried father arrived. He thanked me and took the boy home safely. That evening taught me that a small act of kindness can make a big difference. We should always help people in need.

Section D – Literature

Q9 – Short answers (30–40 words each):

  1. Margie hated school because she studied alone at home with a mechanical teacher. She disliked its frequent tests and the way it displayed marks immediately. She found the old schools with human teachers and children studying together more interesting.
  2. Evelyn learned to sense music through vibrations. Her percussion teacher encouraged her to feel the notes through different parts of her body, such as her skin, cheekbones and feet. With practice, she became a successful musician.
  3. Kezia was afraid of her father because he appeared strict, spoke harshly and often scolded her. She found it difficult to talk to him freely and misunderstood his serious behaviour as a lack of love.
  4. Einstein was curious, imaginative and deeply interested in science. He questioned accepted ideas, thought independently and made important contributions to physics. He also cared about peace and humanity, which made him remarkable beyond his scientific achievements.
  5. The poem “Wind” teaches us to become strong and face difficulties bravely. The wind troubles weak things but becomes a friend to those who are physically and mentally strong. We should build courage and resilience.
  6. At the fair, the child wanted many things, including toys, sweets, flowers, balloons and a ride on the roundabout. However, after getting separated from his parents, he lost interest in all these things and wanted only them.
  7. Toto created trouble through his mischievous behaviour. He tore clothes, broke dishes, damaged household things and disturbed other animals. His playful but destructive actions made him difficult for the family to manage.

Q10 – Long answers (60–80 words each):

  1. In Margie’s time, children studied individually at home with mechanical teachers. Lessons appeared on screens and tests were conducted by machines. In old schools, children went to a common building, studied together and were taught by human teachers. Margie found this idea exciting because children could help one another, share experiences and enjoy learning together.
  2. Bismillah Khan was a great shehnai player who brought the instrument to the classical music stage. Through years of practice and dedication, he gave the shehnai respect as a concert instrument. He performed at important national and international events, including the historic occasion of India’s Independence. His music reflected devotion to his art and Indian cultural traditions.
  3. At first, Kezia feared her father because he was strict and rarely showed affection openly. After she had a nightmare, her father comforted her and allowed her to sleep beside him. She realised that he worked hard and loved her, even if he did not express it in a gentle way. Her fear gradually changed into understanding and affection.
  4. Iswaran was a lively storyteller who made ordinary events sound exciting. He used dramatic expressions, gestures, suspense and detailed descriptions. He often paused at important moments to hold the listener’s attention. His stories included surprising twists and exaggerated incidents, making Mahendra listen with great interest.

Q11 – Reference to context: (1) “The Road Not Taken” by Robert Frost. (2) The two roads symbolise choices and decisions in life. (3) The poem highlights that choices shape our journey and that we cannot always experience every possible path.

Q12 – Extended answers (80–100 words each):

  1. Margie: Margie is an eleven-year-old girl who studies at home with a mechanical teacher. She is not happy with her lonely routine and dislikes the tests given by the machine. When she reads about old schools, she becomes curious about children learning together in classrooms. She imagines how enjoyable it must have been to share lessons, laugh and help classmates. Margie’s character shows the importance of friendship and human interaction in education.
  2. Evelyn Glennie: Evelyn Glennie did not allow her hearing impairment to end her dream of becoming a musician. With the guidance of her teacher Ron Forbes, she learned to feel musical vibrations through her body. She practised with discipline, patience and determination and gradually became an internationally recognised percussionist. Her journey proves that physical limitations need not prevent achievement when a person has confidence, hard work and the right guidance.
  3. The Lost Child: At the fair, the child is attracted by toys, sweets, flowers, balloons and the roundabout. He repeatedly asks his parents for these things, but they do not stop for him. When he becomes separated from them, his excitement turns into fear and distress. A kind man offers him the things he had wanted earlier, but the child refuses everything. He cries only for his mother and father, showing that parental love is more valuable to him than material pleasures.
